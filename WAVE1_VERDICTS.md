# Wave 1 final verdicts
These are the exact words of the fresh critics that ran the real build and looked at the pixels.

## score 66 — reference wins
**Biggest gap:** The camera is authored as a locked, symmetric shot but ships as a follow camera, so the composition the code's own comments describe exists only at rest — in play it dollies back 1.4 units (join 0.525 -> 0.471-0.501, floor depth 7.0 -> 8.4) and slides laterally off the oven, and on iPhone portrait it never recovers.

**Observed:**
Ran the build clean (vite 8.2, 0 console errors on all four profiles, 60fps, worst frame 16.7ms). Captured: shots/critic-p01-camera-r4/desktop and shots/critic-p01-camera-r4b/{iphone-portrait,iphone-landscape,ipad-landscape} (the harness rm -rf's its out dir per run, so the non-desktop profiles landed in the -r4b sibling).

The core camera IDEA is right and I want to say that before I take it apart: 22.5 deg pitch, near-frontal, aimed down the room at a back wall with a stone pizza oven and live fire, ochre stucco, honey beams, flagstone floor in perspective, raked side walls with a round-window door left and a copper pan rack right. That is the reference's shot, not an isometric box. iPad 1194x834 01-opening is the best frame in the set and it is genuinely close.

iPad landscape (best): oven arch dead centre, both team counters and both Toads fully in frame, side wedges at both edges, floor runs to the bottom edge. Telemetry at t=20.0: join 0.522, backWall 0.909, vdr 1.94. Faults: the bottom ~28% is a flagstone plateau broken only by two tables that the bottom edge slices in half, so the front rank contributes plank texture and no readable props; and at t0016s the thumb cluster sits on top of a chef at x~1000-1130, y~640-834 — you can see a hat and nothing else.

Desktop 1440x900: same shot, weaker. Telemetry t=3.0 shows the camera fully dollied out — camZ 15.38 vs restZ 13.99, join 0.478, floorDepth 8.39, vdr 2.01. That is the exact defect the file header claims round 6 fixed; the "measured at rest" table in cameraRig.ts is not what ships during play. 90-late is the worst landscape frame: the pink counter is chopped by the left edge, the oven has slid to ~0.39 of frame width, and a dead ochre band opens on the right — my 3x3 edge-density grid puts that top-right cell at 0.191, the lowest cell measured anywhere including all reference cells (ref right column: 0.243-0.253). Desktop t0016s middle row measures 0.289/0.330/0.270 against the reference's 0.391/0.378/0.374 and 0.412/0.416/0.369. The frame is under-filled.

iPhone landscape 852x393: densest of ours (0.419/0.451/0.335 middle row) and edge-to-edge, but the frame is so short the back wall gets 0.677-0.728 of width, the Toads are half-hidden behind the order bubbles, and the thumb cluster owns the bottom-right quadrant of play.

iPhone portrait 393x852 — this is where it falls apart, and it is the profile the brief singles out. backWallFrac is 1.000 in BOTH samples: the wall fills the entire frame width, so there are zero side wedges. No door, no pan rack, no raked wall. The room does not read as a room, it reads as a slot. visibleDepthRatio 2.51-2.54 against the reference's 1.75-1.81 — 40% over, a visibly wider, more distorted recession than the reference has anywhere. halfWidthAtChef is 1.72-2.78 against a room the chef roams +/-6.1 of, so the pan has to swing hard and CENTRE_MAX_TALL = 1.0 lets the room centre travel a full halfWidth: at t0015s the oven arch is jammed against the RIGHT edge (fire-glow p90 reaches 0.96 of frame width; the reference's fire never passes 0.83), the pink counter is cropped at the left edge and the green counter with its Toad is entirely off-screen. At t0018s the oven sits at ~0.64 and the green station is gone again. So on the primary phone orientation you routinely cannot see one of the two team stations you are cooking for. Above that, a flat ochre band from y~55 to y~155 (11% of frame height) carries no information; below, ~30% is empty flagstone under the buttons. Actual play band: roughly 19% of the screen.

Motion across the timed frames is real — chefs translate, carry poses change, the plate tower and pan appear, the score moves 0 -> 10, the clock runs 2:59 -> 2:40. That is not the problem. The problem is that the camera moves too.

One more thing: cameraRig.ts emits a warnings array and it was EMPTY on all eight samples, including portrait at vdr 2.54 and desktop at join 0.478. Every threshold is phrased as "outside its own dolly range" / "off target at rest", so the rig grades itself against itself rather than against the reference numbers it quotes. That is why these regressions shipped.

**Fixes, in priority order:**
1. src/view/cameraRig.ts: CENTRE_MAX_TALL = 1.0 (line ~423) lets the room centre travel a full halfWidth on portrait, which is why iphone-portrait/t0015s has the oven arch against the right edge and the green team station entirely off-frame. Drop it to ~0.20, same as landscape, and make the oven arch centre a hard clamp rather than a soft shoulder — the reference is locked-off and never moves it off 0.50.
2. src/view/cameraRig.ts: DOLLY_MAX / DOLLY_MAX_TALL = 1.7 (line ~265) is being fully spent in normal play — every profile's early snapshot shows camZ 1.3-1.4 over restZ, dragging join from the authored 0.525 to 0.471-0.501 and floorDepth from 7.0 to 8.39. Cut to ~0.5 and stop buying feet-in-frame with a dolly; instead let the player crop like a bot (reuse CROWD_SINK) or slide the look-at forward, both of which leave the join alone.
3. src/view/cameraRig.ts FRONT_CROP = 3.0 / FRONT_CROP_TALL = 1.8: the bottom edge lands at bottomEdgeZ 8.0-9.4, which slices the row-9 bench tables lengthwise on desktop, iPad and portrait, so the front rank of props reads as plank grain. Stations occupy rows 1,3,5,7,9 — snap the bottom edge to the LANE between rows (z 8.5 or 10.0), never through a row, so the last visible table is whole and carries its trays.
4. src/view/cameraRig.ts HALF_WIDTH_MIN = 3.8 (line ~305): portrait solves to backWallFrac 1.000 in both samples, i.e. zero side-wall wedges. The reference gives ~8% of frame width to the round-window door and ~8% to the copper pan rack, and those wedges are what make the shot read as a room rather than a vertical slot. Raise until backWallFrac lands ~0.85 on 0.461 aspect, and accept the lens cost — visibleDepthRatio there is already 2.51-2.54 against the reference's 1.75-1.81 and needs to come DOWN anyway.
5. src/view/cameraRig.ts describe(): every entry in the warnings array is graded against the rig's own dolly range or its own rest target, so warnings came back [] on all eight samples while portrait sat 40% over the reference depth ratio and desktop sat 0.047 of frame height off the authored join. Re-express the checks against the reference constants (vdr 1.75-1.81, join 0.526, backWall 0.84) evaluated at the LIVE camera, not at rest, and have tools/shoot.mjs surface them in report.json as failures.
6. src/ui/hud.ts + src/view/cameraRig.ts: the thumb clusters occupy roughly the bottom-right 22% of both phone frames and the framing solve reserves nothing for them — iphone-landscape/t0016s and ipad-landscape/t0016s both have a chef almost entirely behind a button. Feed a bottom/right safe-area inset into the solve so the join and crop are computed against the UNOBSTRUCTED viewport, and keep the follow target out of the covered wedge.
7. src/domain/kitchen.ts: even with the crop fixed, the middle-row edge density is 0.289/0.330/0.270 against the reference's 0.39-0.42 across the board. The reference dresses every surface — stockpot, ladle, salt and pepper, a pie, a blue bowl, tall plate stacks, a sink. Add set-dressing props to the open lanes and to the row-7/row-9 tables so the lower half of the frame carries information the camera can actually frame.
8. src/view/materials.ts: room median saturation is 0.52-0.55 against the reference's 0.37-0.41 while food p95 matches at ~0.88-0.95. Desaturate the wall stucco, the bench wood and the flagstone by roughly 20-25% so the food is the most saturated thing on screen by the margin the reference maintains.

## score 68 — reference wins
**Biggest gap:** A single global triplanar grime shader (`applyCeilingOcclusion` in materials.ts, reached by every `toon()` call) smears ±15% low-frequency blotch across characters, props, walls and floor with a hue-shifting blue-channel bias, so where the reference is clean flat albedo with crisp bevelled edges, ours is a uniformly dirty, mushy, matte approximation that falls apart the moment you look at it above thumbnail size.

**Observed:**
Desktop and iPad landscape (01-opening, t0006, t0010, t0016, t0019, 90-late): the structural read is genuinely close to the reference. Low near-frontal camera onto a real back wall, ochre stucco, honey timber posts, a pale stone chimney breast with a lit pizza oven dead centre, pink counter left / green counter right with a Toad-alike behind each, a staggered grid of low knee-height benches out on open floor, side walls angled in at both edges with a copper pan rack right and a door left. Prop density is high and correct: trays of tomato/lettuce/bacon, stockpot, ladle, salt and pepper, sink basin, plate stacks, pancake towers, a wooden butter churn. Order bubbles are big, white, wordless, with pink/green awnings behind them. HUD is three dark translucent pills and nothing else. Motion evidence across the timed frames is real: characters change position and pose every frame, there are run cycles with swinging arms, chop particles, a comedy plate tower carried in t0016, tick marks appearing on order icons, score 0 to 10. Zero console errors across all profiles; render cost 4.2 to 5.4ms.

Then you crop to 100% and it collapses. Every surface in the room carries a soft cloudy stain. The red mouse's apron has grey-pink smears through it; the green frog's head has darker green blotches; the yellow duck's body is streaked. The floor is not warm cream stone, it is olive-khaki mush: each flag is a field of big soft grey clouds with joint lines that are 2-3px of blurred low-contrast grey with no bevel and no bloom, so the flag grid barely resolves. Measured high-frequency energy on a floor patch: reference 13.9 RMS, ours 9.1 — the reference has 50% more crisp detail and ours has more low-frequency slop. The timber posts are dark muddy brown with the same blotching, so the wall/timber value separation that gives the reference its architecture is gone; our posts read as more wall. Props have almost no form shading: the stockpot is a flat grey cylinder at one value; the awnings are flat dusty rose with a diagonal hatch and hard paper scallops. Chopped tomato renders as flat red cuboid wedges and bacon as a flat pink parallelogram slab.

The player marker is a defect, not a style. `playerRingTexture` is an additive warm ring at opacity 0.5, scaled by the same `foot` value as the contact shadow rather than sitting inside it, so it blows out over the light floor into a white glowing donut roughly 1.5× the character's width, and it completely overwhelms the 0.7-opacity dark contact shadow underneath. Net read: the player is standing in a puddle of light with no shadow. In iphone-portrait/t0006s the glow arc crosses the bench edge in front of it.

iPhone portrait is materially broken. In 01-opening both team stations are sliced to slivers at the left and right edges and neither Toad's face is visible, yet both order bubbles are on screen with tails pointing at owners you cannot see. In 90-late the camera has drifted far enough that the entire pink counter and its Toad are off-frame while its bubble still floats there, and the two bubbles overlap each other and clip the oven arch. The top 11-13% of every portrait frame is a slab of empty ochre wall with nothing in it but the HUD pills — dead space, which REFERENCE.md calls a defect. The bottom edge cuts a bench mid-object and drops three flat translucent touch circles over the play area with no diegetic treatment.

iPhone landscape: order bubbles are so large they cover the entire left counter and bite into the oven; characters read at roughly 10% of screen height, below the reference's 15%, and their silhouettes stop being identifiable.

**Fixes, in priority order:**
1. src/view/materials.ts:1060 `applyCeilingOcclusion` — gate the triplanar mottle off characters entirely, the same way `isFood` already gates it. Right now every body part goes through `toon()` at line 1421 and gets the full room grime. Add a third flag (`isCharacter`) and compile the mottle block out, exactly as the food path does. Reference characters carry zero surface noise; ours are the only cartoon animals in this genre with a damp problem.
2. src/view/materials.ts:772 `MOTTLE_GAIN = [0.19, 0.23, 0.31]` — the blue channel is modulated 63% harder than red, so the noise shifts hue and saturation rather than value. Make it monochrome and halve it: `[0.10, 0.10, 0.10]`. Noise that changes chroma is dirt; noise that changes value is texture.
3. src/view/textures.ts:88 `stoneFloorTexture` — the floor is the largest surface in the frame and it is the mushiest. Raise `px` from 128 to 256, cut the per-flag cloud mottle amplitude to about a third, and actually implement the bevel the comment at line 107 says it wants: a 1px joint darker than the flag plus a 1px pale bloom on the lower-right lip of each flag. Measured, the reference floor carries 13.9 RMS of high-frequency detail against our 9.1 — we are blurrier where we should be crisper.
4. src/view/textures.ts:543 `timberTexture` — strip the dark blotching from the posts and beams and replace it with straight fine vertical grain plus a bright top bevel. The reference gets its architecture from three separated values (pale mustard wall / mid honey timber / near-white stone); ours collapses timber into wall because both are blotched brown at similar value. Raise the timber base value roughly one stop while you are in there.
5. src/view/characters.ts:912 and :3078 — the player ring is scaled by the same `foot` value as the contact shadow, so the comment at line 919 claiming it 'sits INSIDE the contact shadow' is false in the render. Multiply the ring scale by about 0.45 of `foot`, drop `opacity` from 0.5 to about 0.22, and warm-tint it lower (currently RGB 255,232,176 additive at 0.5 clips to white on a light floor). It must never be brighter than the brightest food pixel.
6. src/view/characters.ts:3065 — with the ring fixed, the contact shadow needs to actually be visible under the feet. Concentrate it: shrink the ellipse toward the foot placement and raise centre opacity, so it reads as two feet touching stone rather than a wide grey wash. The reference gives every one of its eight bodies a small tight dark pool.
7. src/view/materials.ts:1471 `metal` / :1502 `glazed` — extend the tier system to wood and ceramic form shading, or add a cheap vertical value ramp to cylindrical props. The stockpot, the churn and the plate stacks currently render at a single value and read as cardboard; the reference's pot has a clear bright-shoulder-to-dark-base gradient that costs nothing and sells the whole prop.
8. src/view/cameraRig.ts — portrait framing is the one place the composition genuinely fails. Clamp the camera so both team counters and both Toads stay inside the frame at all times in portrait, and hide or dock an order bubble whose owning station has left the viewport (shots/critic-p02-art-r4/iphone-portrait/90-late.jpg shows a bubble tail pointing at nothing). Also push the horizon up so the 11-13% dead ochre band under the HUD is filled with room instead of blank wall.
9. The chopped-food meshes need replacing. Chopped tomato is currently flat red cuboid wedges and bacon a flat pink parallelogram; find them in src/view/world.ts and give them rounded, bevelled forms with an occlusion contact into the tray, the way whole tomatoes already get at world.ts:2690. Food is the point and cut food is what the player looks at most.

## score 74 — reference wins
**Biggest gap:** The set spends its brightness and chroma budget on architecture instead of on food — the oven mouth measures V 0.93 / S 0.89 dead centre frame against the reference's V 0.40 / S 0.76, and the ochre wall runs luma 126-138 against the reference's 99-100 — so at thumbnail the eye lands on the oven, then the wall, and only third on the tomatoes, inverting the one rule the reference composition is built on.

**Observed:**
STRUCTURE IS RIGHT, AND THAT IS REAL. Every profile shows a low near-frontal camera onto a back wall: ochre stucco, honey timber posts, pale stone chimney breast, arched oven dead centre, warm stone flags in strong perspective, a door with a round window at the left edge, a rack of copper pans at the right, pink counter left / green counter right with Toad-alikes behind them, and low knee-height plank benches scattered on open floor. Characters are never occluded by furniture. The white ceramic trays with tomatoes, lettuce and bacon are the best-read objects in the game. This is not a walled isometric box any more.

WHAT IS WRONG, WITHOUT CHARITY.

1. The oven is the loudest object in the game. Measured on ipad-landscape/90-late over a region on the oven mouth: rgb(189,108,49), and a centre probe hits rgb(236,112,26) — V 0.93, S 0.89. The same probe on refs/dash-and-dine-01 returns rgb(102,32,24), V 0.40, S 0.76. Ours is more than twice the value. The interior is a full-arch orange gradient over a wood-grain back panel with three flat orange lozenges standing in for pizzas, plus a bank of red-lit vents below. The reference gives a dark cavity, a small ember bar, and two modelled pizzas with crust rim and sauce.

2. The back wall is 30% too bright and asymmetric. Region averages: reference wall luma 99 (left) / 100 (right), V 0.59, S 0.87. Ours: 126 (left) / 138 (right), V 0.68-0.74. The reference goes deep-and-saturated; ours goes light-and-saturated, which is the loud combination. Our chimney is luma 195 to the reference's 178, so the entire back band of our room sits bright 126-195 where the reference spans 100-178. The 10% left/right wall imbalance also drags the eye off the play area toward the right frame edge.

3. Half the bench tops are value-dead. Seven chopping boards (KITCHEN_MAP 'X') sit as dark chocolate rectangles on honey benches — same hue, roughly 15% value separation. At thumbnail in the side-by-side they read as holes or burn marks, not props. In ipad/01-opening I count four bench tops carrying only a board plus a grey shaker, and two carrying nothing. The reference never places a brown object on a brown bench: every prop it owns is white ceramic, steel, or a saturated hue.

4. The right half of the room is measurably underdressed. KITCHEN_MAP cells x2-5 carry nine dressed cells; x9-13 carry one board, one bacon, one bin, one tomato, one lettuce and two counters. It shows: on ipad/90-late there is an unbroken grey floor field roughly 230x360px between the green counter and the bottom-right bench, and a second one at bottom-centre. Rows 2, 4, 6 and 8 are fully clear by design, which produces three horizontal grey stripes running edge-to-edge — the reference has no horizontal band of its lower two thirds that is empty across its full width.

5. iPhone portrait loses the room. In t0006s and t0016s the top ~150px (about 18% of the frame) is featureless ochre wall plus HUD with zero architecture in it. Both team counters are sliced to slivers at the frame edges, the door and the pan rack are gone, and the bottom-right quadrant is bare floor under the touch buttons. From t0019s onward the camera drifts with the player, so by 90-late the oven is off-centre-left and the right half is a formless mix of counter fragment, wall clock and floor. Prop count in frame drops to roughly eight.

6. Order bubbles occlude their own source. On iphone-landscape/90-late and t0009s the two bubbles completely cover both Toads' heads — you cannot see who is ordering. On iphone-portrait/t0016s, t0019s and 90-late a third order chip spawns at top-left, is clipped by the left frame edge, overlaps the primary bubble, and collides with the HUD score pill. It is also a circle where everything else is a speech bubble, so it reads as a bug.

7. Prop silhouettes are mushy. Our stockpot is a grey dome with a nub; the reference's has handles, a domed lid and a clean elliptical rim. Our plate stacks are smooth grey cylinders; the reference's show individual plate edges. Our sink is a pale blue rounded box. A blue ovoid prop appears on at least three benches and I cannot identify it at any size — that is an outright failure of the 200ms read test.

8. The floor loses its perspective cue. Joint contrast falls below about 10 luma in the near field, so the flags dissolve into a wash. Our mid-floor also measures S 0.52 against the reference's 0.42 — browner and more chromatic where it should be neutral. There is a brighter unmotivated wedge at lower-left and a darker patch centre-right that corresponds to no object.

9. The set has no life. Across desktop t0005 / t0010 / t0016 the fire lozenges are pixel-identical, the copper pans never swing, the stockpot never steams. Only characters and one dust puff move.

10. Contact shadows read as halos in places — the red chef on ipad/90-late sits on a pale grey ring that is lighter than the floor around it, not a soft dark ellipse.
